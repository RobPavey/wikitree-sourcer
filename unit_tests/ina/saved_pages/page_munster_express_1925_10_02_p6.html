IRISH SEED POTATOES

EQUAL TO BEST IMPORTED. A large gathering of fanners and others interested in agriculture visited the Munster Agricultural Institute, where they were received by Mr. W. D. Davidson, the Department' s potato expert, who took them to the experimental plots where many varieties of potatoes were growing, and the result of the experiments were lucidly _explained.. Mr. Davidson said from the tests conducted this season two definite facts were established :— ¦ ; (-1) That it was not necessary to go butside Ireland to produce the best seed. (2) That potatoes could be grown as well in Co. Cork as in any part of the world.

Amongst other. facts demonstrated were that the variety Epicure, grown from seed purchased in Scotland and from seed purchased in Athlone, gave the following yields per statute acre :— Athlone seed .20 tons 7 cwts. Scottish seed 19 tons 17 cwts. A similar test with British Queens gave the following yields ; . Athlone seed 20 tons 7 cwts. Scottish seed ... . ... 18 tons 12 cwts. Grown a second year these crops _yielded : Athlone seed 20 tons 13 cwts: Scottish seed .... ... 21 tons .3 cwts. An experiment was made with the variety Kerr_'s Pink, with . Scottish and Donegal seed, and the yields were :

Scottish seed ... ... 16 tons 7 cwts. Donegal seed ... ... 15 tons 16 cwts. The Donegal seed sown a second year produced 18 tons 17 cwts. per statute acre, and for a third year, 15 tons 6 cwts. The Farm Manager (Mr. McCall) considered that the excellent yields were due to the following: (1) the land had Jain for a long number of years in grass and was only broken up two years ago; (2) the crop was Well manured with farmyard and 10 cwts. of the usual mixture of artificials per statute acre; (3) the crop had been sprayed thrco times. EGG EXPORTS

THE NEW. REGULATIONS. Last year tlie eggs exported from tlie Free State represented £3,079,200 in value. In the first six months of this year thei value of our egg exports was £1,%7,!JJO, or £64,000 more than in the corresponding period of 1924. That this trade is capable of expansion is apparent from the fact that Great Britain requires every year £15,500,000 worth of imported eggs. The share of that trade captured by Ireland is not nearly as

great as it might be. In the past both producers and exporters in Ireland were entirely too negligent in the handling and packing of eggs. The producer hoarded them for the local merchant, wiio in turn hoarded them for somebody else, who ultimately consigned them to an exporter. No wonder it used to be said that " the Irish fresh egg was a stale ess " when it reached the breakfast table of the British consumer. As a result of the long delay in marketing and the inefficient system adopted, Irish eggs had obtained a bad reputation. Last July

an Act regulating the export of eggs was passed by the Oireachtas. Under tlie provisions of the Act all eggs exported from the Free State shall have been tested, graded, and packed in accordance with prescribed regulations, and all exporters shall ba registered. After October * 1, Thursday last, no package of eggs may be exported, unless such package; is of . standard pattern, is. marked .in the,prescribed manner, and is consigned direct from premises registered under the Act. These provisions do not apply . t& eggs: exported-by means of

the parcel post, or to consignments the total gross weight of which does not exceed the mamimum weight allowed to be sent by parcel post, or eggs not exceeding ten dozens exported for _tho purpose of incubation, or eggs exported under a special licence. Non-compliance with the Act renders the offender liable, on a first conviction, to a fine of £20, and, in the case of a second offence, £50, or, at the discretion of the Court, to imprisonment for six months, or to botli fine and imprisonment. Through tlie enforcement of the Act a great improvement in the egg trade should be effected.
